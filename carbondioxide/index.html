01 / Physical observations

Breath, carbon & deep time

Living lungs exchange gases. Carbon moves through living things, oceans, rocks, and the atmosphere. These are physical processes we can investigate.

Observe

Lungs exhale CO 2

Respiration releases carbon dioxide. Each CO 2 molecule contains one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms . We can name the molecule, describe its structure, and measure CO 2 in exhaled air.

People also inhale air containing some CO 2 . Exhaled breath generally contains a higher concentration because metabolism produces CO 2 that blood carries to the lungs.

Locate in time

Earth's carbon cycle through deep time

Earth formed approximately 4.54 billion years ago . The infographic traces the Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic, and Phanerozoic eons, showing how carbon has participated in geology and life over billions of years.

The present carbon cycle includes photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, ocean exchange, and geological storage. Human activities also move carbon from long-term stores into the atmosphere.

Distinguish

A natural molecule; measurable changes

CO 2 is essential to many natural processes. That does not mean every atmospheric concentration or added emission has the same effect. Assessing sustainability requires quantities, time scales, and consequences—not simply labeling a molecule good or bad.

02 / People and authorized action

Gender equality, brain health & consent

Equality concerns opportunities and voice. Brain health concerns living people. Consent determines whether particular human actions are authorized.

Equality

SDG 5: Gender Equality

Equal rights, opportunities and voice for all genders. Sustainable systems include the people whose work, health, agency, and futures are affected by decisions.

Neuroscience

Glymphatic flow and sleep

Glymphatic flow describes a proposed and actively studied pathway for fluid movement and waste clearance in the brain. Research connects sleep with changes in brain-fluid dynamics and waste clearance; the exact mechanisms and their clinical significance continue to be investigated.

Sleep supports many aspects of brain health, learning, and longer-term function. The illustration conveys a scientific topic, rather than a complete clinical model.

Authorization

Consent constrains authorized action

Clear, ongoing, informed agreement is central to ethical research, intervention, and use. A yes is meaningful only when it is voluntary and specific enough for the action involved. A no must be respected. Consent can be withdrawn.

03 / Collective practice

Equality, systems & true value

Cooperation takes place among living people and within living ecosystems. Money can count transactions; it cannot by itself measure planetary health.

Equality

UN Women

Empower women. Advance equality. Build a better world for all. This principle concerns rights, participation, and opportunities in the systems we build and maintain.

Application

Sustainable systems

Collaboration across people, fields, and communities can help build healthier and more resilient systems. The infographic's 1 + 1 + 1 expresses an invitation to work together— people + planet + practice —rather than a literal ecological measurement.

Practical outcomes still require observation: for example, air quality, habitat conditions, resource use, wellbeing, and how decisions affect people.

Compare claims

≠ Abstract valuation

Two pennies have a combined monetary value of $0.02 . That arithmetic is correct. But $0.02 is not equal to the value of a healthy Earth system : an ecosystem's processes, species, relationships, and stability cannot be captured by that amount.

Economic tools can be useful for particular decisions, yet a monetary figure is neither a complete ecological description nor a substitute for physical evidence.

04 / Reading Level Test

Formalize the written claim.

CO₂ is exhaled.

RLT begins with a reading point: the words as written, the conventions that determine how to read them, the relationships proved under stated assumptions, and the physical evidence relevant to the claim.

The formula CO₂ identifies one carbon and two oxygen atoms. The arithmetic can be checked formally; the observation that people exhale carbon dioxide calls for physical evidence. Both belong in an accountable specification.

05 / Reading points

Nonfiction vs Fiction.

Reading a claim accurately includes identifying what is written, how its language is being used, and what evidence can support its physical interpretation.

Nonfiction / A book about carbon dioxide

The Story of CO₂ Is the Story of Everything

Peter Brannen · How Carbon Dioxide Made Our World · 2025

Brannen traces carbon dioxide through Earth's long history, connecting geological change, climate, and life. It extends the deep-time reading on this page: CO₂ is present in breath now, while carbon-containing processes also connect organisms, oceans, rocks, and atmosphere across immense timescales.

Reading distinction / Interpretation and verification

What does “everything” mean?

The book title is expansive language, not a formal assertion that every object or event is CO₂. RLT retains an expression as written and asks which specific consequences can be proved and which accounts of the physical world require observation.

This page offers a starting example, not a complete account of carbon dioxide or of the book.

Optimizing sustainability: measurable ecosystems, living brains and consent —not abstract monetary valuation.
